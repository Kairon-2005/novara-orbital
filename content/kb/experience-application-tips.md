# Recurring Community Advice for NUS/NTU Applicants

## Common application mistakes people warn about

- **Applying late within a "rolling-ish" window.** Many believe earlier submission within the October–February/March window improves odds and (more verifiably) gives earlier housing/scholarship access. Waiting for "one more test score" is a commonly regretted delay.
- **Treating minimums as targets.** Published minimum requirements (English scores, "good pass" wording) are far below the competitive reality for popular courses; people who applied at the minimum and were rejected post about it often.
- **Sloppy paperwork.** Mismatched names across documents, unverified transcripts (CSSD/CHESICC for Gaokao), missing notarised translations, and uploaded files that don't match what was declared all cause delays or quiet rejections in anecdotes.
- **Choice order mistakes.** People report regret over "wasting" top choices on long-shot courses without a realistic safety, or conversely listing a course they didn't want and feeling locked in after an offer.
- **Ignoring subject prerequisites.** Strong totals with the wrong subject combination (no HL/H2 maths for computing, no physics for engineering) is a classic rejection story.
- **Assuming conditional offers exist everywhere.** NTU in particular is reported not to give predicted-grade offers for several qualifications — students plan a gap cycle they didn't expect.

## Essay / activity section tips (community consensus)

- Reflection beats listing: describe 2–3 activities with what you did, what changed, and what you learned, rather than ten one-liners.
- Tie activities to the chosen major — admissions essays/ABA responses are read for "why this course," and generic prestige-seeking statements are said to read poorly.
- Honesty and a natural voice over template language; recycled agent-written statements are reportedly easy to spot.
- For NUS scholarship/ABA-style short questions, people advise preparing concise stories mapped to values like innovation, resilience, excellence, respect, integrity — the prompts often ask for exactly that.
- Keep records: some courses/scholarships ask follow-up questions or interview you on what you wrote — anything you can't speak to fluently will hurt.

## Scholarship interview experiences (NUS Merit / ASEAN / Nanyang)

- Format reported as a **panel of 2–3 interviewers (often professors), 15–30 minutes**, sometimes preceded by a short on-the-spot essay; some cycles ran group discussions.
- Recurring questions: introduce yourself; why this course and this university; biggest achievement/failure; current-affairs opinions; "why should we pick you over equally qualified candidates"; what you'd do for the school community.
- Tips repeated by past candidates: know the scholarship's non-monetary benefits and obligations; don't be intimidated by polished co-interviewees; admit what you don't know rather than bluff; prepare a question or two to ask back.
- Reality-check: full scholarships (Nanyang Scholarship, NUS Merit/Global Merit, ASEAN UG) are described as extremely competitive for internationals; many strong admits get no interview at all. Tuition Grant (with its ~3-year Singapore work bond) is the more common funding route, and the bond's implications are a frequent discussion topic in Chinese-language forums.

## Choosing between NUS and NTU (when holding both offers)

- **Academics/pace:** NUS is widely described as more intense — denser assessment, faster pace, very current CS syllabus; NTU as somewhat more relaxed early on, leaving room for personal projects. Both views are anecdotal and programme-dependent.
- **Location:** NUS is closer to the city (Kent Ridge); NTU's Jurong campus is remote ("a forest" in memes) but compensates with more on-campus housing supply and a self-contained campus life.
- **Housing:** NTU is commonly said to guarantee on-campus housing for roughly the first two years; NUS hall places beyond year one are competitive — a genuine decision factor for internationals.
- **Course-level differences** matter more than brand: people advise comparing the actual curriculum, double-degree/second-major options (NTU's business+accountancy combos, NUS's NOC/overseas programmes get frequent praise), and cohort size.
- Among Chinese applicants, NUS's higher overall ranking is often the tiebreaker for returnees concerned with 名气 (name recognition) at home; others counter that employers in Singapore treat the two as peers.

## Accommodation and cost gotchas for internationals

- **Apply for housing immediately on (or before) acceptance** — application windows can open around February–April and close before some internationals even receive offers; missing the window pushes you into private rentals. Waitlists do move through July, but run a parallel private search.
- Reported on-campus costs: roughly **SGD 400–1,200/month** depending on hall/room type (NTU generally cheaper than NUS); off-campus room rentals commonly **SGD 800–1,800+/month** post-2022 rent surge, with whole-unit private condos far above that.
- All-in annual budgets cited by Chinese students: roughly **RMB 250k–350k/year** (tuition after no grant + housing + living) — significantly less with Tuition Grant, which most discuss carefully because of the work bond.
- Gotchas mentioned repeatedly: hall offers can be room-type lottery; air-conditioning and meal-plan charges add up; agent fees and 1–2 month deposits for off-campus leases; some landlords avoid short leases aligned to semesters; cooking restrictions in halls.
- Budget extras people forget: student's pass fees, medical insurance, initial settling-in costs, and flights during result-season uncertainty.

## Honest uncertainty

- Costs and housing policies move quickly (rents spiked sharply in recent years and have been volatile since); figures above are rough community recollections spanning several cycles.
- Scholarship structures, interview formats, and Tuition Grant terms change — verify against NUS OAM, NTU admissions/scholarship pages, and MOE TGonline before advising any applicant.
