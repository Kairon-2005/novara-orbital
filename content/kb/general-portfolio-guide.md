# Building an Admission Portfolio

How to build and document extracurricular evidence. Organised around Novara's five assessment dimensions so retrieved guidance maps directly onto assessment gaps.

## Principles

- **Depth beats breadth.** Two activities sustained for 2+ years with measurable outcomes outweigh eight one-off memberships. Admissions readers scan for trajectory: joined → contributed → led → created.
- **Academics first.** For NUS/NTU, grades and subject prerequisites dominate; the portfolio differentiates among academically qualified applicants and drives scholarships and aptitude-based admissions. A portfolio never rescues missing prerequisites.
- **Evidence or it didn't happen.** Certificates, publications, repositories, photos, press, letters — collect them at the time, not two years later.

## Dimension 1 — Academic Strength

- Target the grade profile of your programme (see NUS IGP / NTU competitiveness notes), not the minimum requirements.
- Subject olympiads are the highest-signal academic extracurricular: AMC → AIME chain, SMO, SPhO, SChO, SBO, NOI (informatics). Regional rounds already signal rigour; national/international medals are differentiators.
- Structured external coursework (AP exams beyond school requirements, university MOOCs with certificates, NUS/NTU outreach programmes for school students) shows initiative within academics.

## Dimension 2 — Programme Fit

- Build a visible thread between activities and the target programme. CS/AI/Data: personal coding projects with public repositories, hackathons, Kaggle, app deployments with real users. Business: case competitions (e.g. school-level DECA-style events), a small venture with accounts, market-research projects. Engineering: robotics (FRC/VEX), maker projects, science fairs (SSEF in Singapore).
- One substantial capstone project told well (problem → approach → result → users/impact) is worth more than five tutorials-following clones.
- Read the actual curriculum of the target programme and mirror its vocabulary when describing your work.

## Dimension 3 — Evidence Portfolio

- Maintain a single evidence folder: every certificate, transcript, score report, photo of you presenting, link to code/writing, reference letter — named `YYYY-MM-activity-type.ext`.
- Quantify everything at collection time: hours, team size, users, money raised, rank, percentile.
- For digital work, permanence matters: a GitHub repo with commit history, a published blog post, or a recorded performance is independently verifiable; a private file is not.

## Dimension 4 — Communication & Storytelling

- Activities that produce communication evidence: Model UN, debate, school publications, teaching/tutoring juniors, conference presentations, a technical blog.
- Bilingual ability is an asset — document it (translation volunteering, bilingual publications) rather than just claiming it.
- Practice the 90-second version of each major activity; interviews and essays both draw on it.

## Dimension 5 — Initiative & Impact

- The question to answer: what exists because of you that wouldn't otherwise? Founding a club, starting a community service project, organising an event, shipping something used by others.
- Service should be sustained and specific (one cause, repeated involvement, growing responsibility), not a scatter of one-day events.
- Leadership is contribution made visible: "organised", "founded", "grew membership from X to Y" — with a named outcome.

## Common mistakes

- Hoarding titles (member of 10 clubs) with no outcomes attached.
- Starting portfolio work in the final year — too late to show sustained engagement; start 2-3 years out.
- Paying for prestige-sounding programmes with no selective admission or real output; reviewers discount these.
- Letting the portfolio compromise grades — for NUS/NTU specifically, this trade is almost always negative.
- Failing to collect evidence at the time; reconstructing it later is hard and looks thin.

## Calendar anchors (Singapore context)

- Competition season clusters: SMO (May–Jun), AMC (Nov), NOI (Mar), SSEF (Jan–Apr), SYPT (around Mar).
- DSA (for secondary/JC admission via talent) opens around May, closes September — relevant to students still moving through the Singapore school system.
- Plan one portfolio "peak" per semester; avoid stacking everything into exam months.
