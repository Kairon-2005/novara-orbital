> **Community-sourced, anecdotal; verify against official sources.** Everything below is paraphrased from public forum discussions (Zhihu, Gter/寄托, 1point3acres, Reddit) by past applicants. Numbers are what individuals reported, not official cutoffs, and admissions standards shift year to year.

# Applying to NUS/NTU from China via Gaokao — Community Experience Themes

## What score levels people report

- The most consistent community heuristic: a competitive Gaokao score sits roughly **100–150 points above the provincial first-tier line (一本线)** for science-track students, with NUS skewing toward the top of that range and NTU sometimes cited slightly lower (~80–120 above).
- Several recent threads claim admitted students in strong provinces mostly landed at **985-university level scores or above** — commonly framed as "660+/750" or "top 0.5–1% of the province" for NUS. Treat these as self-reported anecdotes from a small, success-biased sample.
- Popular majors are reported as much harder: Computing, Medicine, Law, and Business at NUS reportedly demand near-Tsinghua/PKU-level scores, while less oversubscribed engineering or science programmes are described as reachable at the lower end of the range.
- NTU's published baseline (average ≥80% of total Gaokao score) is widely described in forums as a **floor, not a realistic admit level** — people report rejections well above it.
- Applicants repeatedly note that competition profile matters at the margin: one widely shared (unverified) claim is that most successfully admitted Gaokao applicants to NUS Computing had provincial-level Olympiad awards or research projects. Even if the exact figure is dubious, the theme — "score alone is often not enough for CS" — recurs constantly.

## English test prep themes

- Commonly reported targets: **IELTS 6.5+ (writing 6.0–6.5)** or **TOEFL ~90–92+** for comfort; some applicants report offers with IELTS 6.0 for less competitive programmes, but most advise aiming higher than the published minimum.
- A recurring stress point: students who waited until after Gaokao to take IELTS found test seats scarce in June–July and felt rushed. Common advice is to take the English test in **Gao 2 (year 11) or early Gao 3**, since scores are valid for two years.
- Several students mention SAT/ACT or NTU's optional AST as a way to strengthen a borderline Gaokao profile; opinions differ on whether it materially helped.

## Interview experiences

- NTU interviews (for shortlisted Gaokao applicants, typically in July) are described as **conversational, in English, 10–30 minutes**: self-introduction, why Singapore/NTU, why the major, hobbies, sometimes a light academic or current-affairs question.
- Business and a few other programmes at both schools are reported to have more structured interviews — short career-plan questions, occasional case-style or written components.
- Interviewees consistently say the bar tested is **spoken English fluency and genuine motivation**, not trick questions. People who memorised scripts report it backfiring; those who practised free-form mock interviews felt prepared.
- Some applicants report no interview at all (direct offer or direct rejection) — interview shortlisting itself is treated by the community as a positive signal.

## Timeline stress points

- Application windows open roughly **October–February/March** for the following August intake; current Gao 3 students apply with Gao 1/Gao 2 transcripts before knowing their Gaokao result.
- The single most-cited pressure point: **actual Gaokao scores must be submitted within ~3 days of release** (late June), followed quickly by verification (CSSD/CHESICC reports) and possible July interviews. Students describe this window as chaotic — common advice is to prepare the verification account and documents in advance.
- Decisions for Gaokao-route applicants often land in **July**, sometimes uncomfortably close to (or after) the mainland 提前批/本科批 filing deadlines. Many threads discuss the gamble of listing domestic universities as backup while waiting on Singapore.
- Several people note this route effectively forces a "dual-track" senior year: preparing Gaokao while also doing IELTS and application paperwork, and they warn it costs real revision time.

## Agent vs DIY

- A large share of forum posters say the **application itself is simple enough to DIY**: an online form, transcripts, English score, and a fee — far less essay-heavy than US applications. DIY success stories are common.
- Complaints about agents recur: charging high fees (commonly cited at RMB 30k–100k+) for what is mostly form-filling, pushing students toward "guaranteed-admission" private-school or pathway products when the public-university bar isn't met, and occasionally missing deadlines.
- Where people did find agents useful: families with zero English ability, last-minute post-Gaokao applications, and interview coaching.
- Repeated warning: be skeptical of any agency claiming guaranteed NUS/NTU undergraduate admission or special quota — community consensus is that no such channel exists.

## Honest uncertainty

- Forum samples are heavily success-biased; rejected applicants post less. Score thresholds quoted online vary by province, year, and major, and Singapore universities do not publish Gaokao cutoffs.
- Policies (e.g., interview rounds, AST acceptance, document deadlines) have changed in recent cycles — always re-check the official NUS OAM and NTU admissions pages for the current cycle.
