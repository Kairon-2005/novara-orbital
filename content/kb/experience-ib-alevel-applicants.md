> **Community-sourced, anecdotal; verify against official sources.** This digest paraphrases recurring themes from student forums (r/SGExams, r/nus, r/NTU, Quora, KiasuParents, education blogs). Grade figures are what past applicants and observers report, not official cutoffs.

# IB / A-Level Applicant Experiences for NUS and NTU

## Predicted vs actual grades — a recurring shock

- Multiple international IB/A-Level applicants report being surprised that **NTU does not give conditional offers on predicted grades** — final certified results are required, so May/June IB graduates often end up applying in the *following* cycle. NUS is described as somewhat more flexible for some qualifications, but applicants still report needing finals for confirmation. Always confirm the current cycle's policy.
- IB students from international schools commonly report a **predicted-vs-actual gap of 1–3 points**; those whose offers were anchored to high predicteds describe scrambling when finals dipped. Common advice: target programmes where your *realistic* final score is competitive, not your predicted ceiling.
- Singapore-Cambridge A-Level students (JC route) note results come out in February for August intake, making theirs a fast, compressed application window — about three weeks from results to deadline in many accounts.

## What grade profiles got offers (community-reported)

- **IB:** people commonly cite roughly 37–40 for NUS/NTU Business and Computing, ~38–41 for competitive Engineering, 40+ for Law, and 41–43 (near-perfect) for Medicine — with HL Maths (Analysis & Approaches) at 6–7 treated as near-mandatory for quant-heavy courses. A repeated anecdote pattern: a 40-pointer with the wrong HLs rejected while a 37-pointer with the right HLs got in.
- **A-Level (Singapore JC):** community discussion orbits the official **Indicative Grade Profiles (IGP)** — the 10th percentile of admitted students. For oversubscribed courses (NUS/NTU Computer Science, NUS Business, Law, Medicine) the IGP has sat near the top of the rank-point scale in recent years (roughly AAA/A territory, high-80s out of 90 rank points), and forum users stress that 90% of admits scored *above* that line.
- **International A-Level (e.g., from China or international schools):** anecdotes suggest mostly A*/A profiles among CS/Business admits, with 3 A-Levels + maths essentially expected for quantitative courses. English test scores around IELTS 6.5–7.0 are commonly reported among admitted internationals.
- Caveat repeated in nearly every thread: there is **no official IGP for IB or international qualifications**, so IB applicants are flying with less data; people extrapolate from small samples.

## IGP reality-checks (themes from r/SGExams-type discussions)

- "IGP is the 10th percentile, not the cutoff" is the most repeated correction — meeting the IGP means you're around the *bottom* 10% of last year's admits, not safely in.
- People warn IGP lags reality for fast-rising courses: CS demand spiked in recent cycles, so last year's IGP understated this year's bar.
- Conversely, posters point out the 90th-percentile column shows even top courses admit a range — borderline applicants with strong aptitude-based admissions (ABA) cases, portfolios, or interviews do sometimes get in below the typical profile.
- International applicants are frequently reminded that IGP reflects local A-Level admits; the effective bar for international students on the same course is widely believed (anecdotally) to be higher because international places are limited.

## Profiles that got CS / Business / Engineering offers

- **CS:** typical reported admit profiles combine near-top grades with signals like programming projects, hackathons or olympiad participation, and a coherent "why computing" story for ABA/discretionary review. Plenty of straight-A rejections from NUS CS are reported, reinforcing that demand exceeds places.
- **Business:** interviews/essays feature more often; admits describe leadership CCAs, entrepreneurship-flavoured activities, and confident interview performance as differentiators at similar grades. NTU Business (with its accountancy double options) is described as marginally less cutthroat than NUS BBA in some years, but both are oversubscribed.
- **Engineering:** consistently described as the most forgiving of the three for grades (mid-tier IGP), with specific streams (e.g., NTU's CS-adjacent engineering, biomedical) tighter than core mechanical/civil. Some applicants use less-subscribed engineering programmes as a strategic entry, hoping to transfer — others warn internal transfer to CS is itself very competitive.

## Poly-route experiences

- Polytechnic diploma holders report that competitive NUS/NTU courses effectively expect **GPA ~3.8–3.9+/4.0 for CS and Business**, ~3.5+ for many engineering courses; below ~3.5 the public-university odds are described as slim without exceptional portfolio evidence.
- Recurring positives: relevant diplomas can earn **module exemptions** (shortening the degree), and internships/capstone projects carry real weight in poly applications.
- Recurring frustrations: poly grads feel the route to the most selective courses is statistically narrower than the JC route, and some describe pivoting to SIT/SUSS or overseas options as backup.
- Note for Novara's audience: this route is mostly relevant to internationals already studying in Singapore polytechnics; direct mainland applicants rarely take it.

## Honest uncertainty

- Forum data over-represents successes and Singapore citizens/PRs; international-quota dynamics are opaque and almost everything said about them is inference.
- IGPs, ABA mechanics, and interview requirements change yearly — re-verify against the official NUS OAM and NTU admissions pages each cycle.
