# University Application Playbook

General guidance applicable to most overseas undergraduate applications (Singapore, UK, US, Australia, Hong Kong). Where NUS/NTU differ, this is noted; consult the NUS/NTU-specific documents for exact figures.

## The application timeline (final two school years)

**Penultimate year (e.g. JC1 / IB Year 1 / Grade 11)**
- Jan–Jun: shortlist target universities and programmes; map each programme's subject prerequisites against your current subjects — changing subjects later is costly or impossible.
- Mar–Aug: take English tests (IELTS/TOEFL) and standardized tests (SAT/ACT, AP exams) early so a retake is possible. Scores are typically valid 2 years.
- Jun–Sep: build the activity/evidence portfolio: competitions, research, leadership, projects (see portfolio guide).
- Oct–Dec: draft personal statements and essays; identify and brief recommenders.

**Final year (e.g. JC2 / IB Year 2 / Grade 12)**
- Oct–Mar: application windows. NUS international qualifications: roughly early December to late February. NTU: roughly mid-October to mid-January (Gaokao group) or mid-March (other international groups). UK UCAS: mid-October (Oxbridge/medicine) or end of January. US: November (early) / January (regular).
- Nov–Apr: interviews and admissions tests where applicable.
- Final exams (A-Level Nov, IB Nov/May, Gaokao Jun) — conditional offers depend on these.
- Mar–Jul: outcomes, acceptance, student pass/visa, housing.

Rule of thumb: everything that can be finished before the final year (tests, portfolio, drafts) should be — the final year belongs to exams and applications.

## Personal statement and essays

- Answer the actual prompt. NUS/NTU forms have short essay/activity sections; UK personal statements are programme-focused; US essays are character-focused. One master document, adapted per system — never one generic essay everywhere.
- Structure that works: a concrete opening moment → what you did (specific, quantified) → what it changed in your thinking → why this programme/university is the logical next step.
- Specificity beats adjectives. "Led a 4-person team that built a timetable app used by 200 students" outperforms "passionate and hardworking leader".
- Show fit: reference specific modules, labs, professors, or programme structures. For NUS/NTU, knowing the actual curriculum (e.g. NUS CS focus areas, NTU's REP) signals seriousness.
- Common failure modes: re-listing the CV in prose; spending half the essay on childhood; claiming passion without evidence; exceeding word limits; AI-sounding generic text.
- Iterate: 3+ drafts, with feedback from someone who reads English professionally. Read it aloud.

## Recommendation letters

- Choose recommenders who know your work, not the highest title. A subject teacher who saw you struggle and improve writes a better letter than a principal who can't name your projects.
- Brief your recommender: give a one-page summary of your achievements with them, your target programmes, deadlines, and what themes you'd like reinforced.
- Ask early — at least 6-8 weeks before deadlines. Teachers in application season are overloaded.
- For NUS/NTU undergraduate admission, recommendations are generally optional/not central (academics dominate), but they matter for scholarships (e.g. Nanyang Scholarship, NUS Merit) and aptitude-based admissions.

## Standardized and English tests

- English tests (where required): IELTS Academic or TOEFL iBT. Take by mid-penultimate year; allow one retake window. Check each university's exact minimums — both NUS and NTU publish per-route requirements (Gaokao-route applicants must present one).
- SAT/ACT: not required for most NUS/NTU routes, but can strengthen borderline cases and is required for the American-curriculum route.
- AP: NUS expects ~5 APs (or 3 + SAT/ACT); Calculus BC is effectively compulsory for STEM. NTU expects ≥3 APs at 4–5.
- Plan tests around school exams; never schedule a test in the same month as finals.

## Interviews

- NUS/NTU interview selectively: Gaokao-route shortlists, Medicine, Dentistry, Law, some scholarships, and aptitude-based applicants.
- Preparation: 2-minute self-introduction; why this programme; one project you can discuss in depth for 10 minutes; one current development in the field; questions to ask back.
- Technical programmes may probe maths/logic informally. Honesty about what you don't know, followed by visible reasoning, scores better than bluffing.

## Application hygiene

- One spreadsheet: programme, deadline, requirements, test minimums, documents, status. Check it weekly.
- Documents: passport, transcripts (with certified English translations), test reports, certificates — scanned, named consistently, backed up.
- Submit 1+ week before deadlines; portals fail under last-day load.
- Verification: universities verify original documents at enrolment; any inconsistency between uploaded and original documents can void an offer.
