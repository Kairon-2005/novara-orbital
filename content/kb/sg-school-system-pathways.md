# Singapore School System and Pathways into NUS/NTU

This article maps the Singapore education system for international (e.g. Chinese) students and shows how each route feeds into NUS/NTU undergraduate admissions.

## 1. Entering the system: AEIS / S-AEIS (official facts)

- The **Admissions Exercise for International Students (AEIS)** is MOE's centralised exercise for international students seeking admission to **Primary 2–5 and Secondary 1–3** in mainstream MOE schools for the *following* academic year (source: moe.gov.sg, verified Jun 2026).
- Applications typically open in **July**; centralised tests are held around **September–October**; successful students start school in **January**. (The 2026 AEIS, for 2027 admission, opens July 2026 with tests in Sep/Oct 2026 — dates tentative per MOE.)
- The **Supplementary AEIS (S-AEIS)** is a second-chance exercise (typically held early in the year) for admission to **Primary 2–4 and Secondary 1–2** within the *same* academic year.
- Admission is **not guaranteed**: it depends on test performance and school vacancies; MOE assigns the school, taking declared residential area into account where possible.
- There is no AEIS entry to Primary 6 or Secondary 4 (exam years) or to junior colleges.

**Guidance:** AEIS tests English and Mathematics at the level applied for. English is usually the main hurdle for students from China; most families budget 6–12 months of preparation. Students who cannot clear AEIS often enter private/international schools instead (see Section 4).

## 2. Secondary stage (roughly ages 13–16)

### O-Level track (mainstream)
- Most MOE secondary schools run 4–5 years ending in the **Singapore-Cambridge GCE O-Level** (now transitioning to the Singapore-Cambridge Secondary Education Certificate with subject-based banding; the O-Level remains the reference standard for older cohorts).
- O-Level results determine posting to **Junior College (JC)**, **Millennia Institute (3-year A-Level)**, or **Polytechnic** via the Joint Admissions Exercise (JAE).

### Integrated Programme (IP)
- Top schools (e.g. Raffles Institution, Hwa Chong Institution) run a 6-year **Integrated Programme** that skips O-Levels and leads straight to A-Levels or IB.
- **Guidance:** IP places for international students are scarce and highly competitive; AEIS entrants are placed in mainstream (O-Level) schools, not IP schools.

## 3. Post-secondary routes into NUS/NTU

Both NUS and NTU sort applicants into qualification-based application groups. The four "local qualification" groups at both universities are (official, verified on nus.edu.sg and ntu.edu.sg):

1. **Singapore-Cambridge GCE A-Level**
2. **Polytechnic Diploma from Singapore** (or equivalent local institution)
3. **NUS High School Diploma**
4. **International Baccalaureate (IB) Diploma**

Foreigners with other qualifications (e.g. Gaokao, international school A-Levels taken outside the Singapore-Cambridge system) apply under **International Qualifications for Foreigners**.

### 3a. Junior College → Singapore-Cambridge A-Level (mainstream route)
- 2 years of JC (or 3 years at Millennia Institute) ending in the **Singapore-Cambridge GCE A-Level**.
- Feeds the **Singapore-Cambridge GCE A-Level** application group at NUS/NTU — the largest intake group.
- NUS treats JC/poly/NUS High graduates as "flow-through foreign students", who apply in the local groups rather than as international applicants (official, NUS OAM).
- **Competitiveness (guidance):** popular programmes (Computing, Law, Medicine, Business) typically need close to straight As; check each university's **Indicative Grade Profile (IGP)** for the 10th/90th percentile of admitted grades. Application outcomes are released by about May.

### 3b. Polytechnic diploma route
- 3-year diploma at one of five polytechnics (Singapore Poly, Ngee Ann, Temasek, Nanyang, Republic).
- Feeds the **Polytechnic Diploma from Singapore** application group at NUS/NTU.
- **Competitiveness (guidance):** only a minority of poly graduates progress to NUS/NTU; competitive programmes generally require a **GPA around 3.7–3.9+ out of 4.0**, and the diploma should be in a relevant field. This route is slower (3 years vs 2) but suits students stronger in applied work than in exam-heavy JC study.

### 3c. NUS High School of Mathematics and Science
- 6-year specialised independent school awarding its own **NUS High School Diploma**, accepted by NUS, NTU and overseas universities.
- Feeds the dedicated **NUS High School Diploma** group at both universities.
- **Guidance:** admission to NUS High itself is via selection tests/DSA and is very competitive; intake of international students is small. Graduates are strong candidates for STEM programmes.

### 3d. IB Diploma route
- Schools offering the IB Diploma in Singapore include **ACS (Independent)** and **St Joseph's Institution (SJI)** (MOE-linked schools whose IB places for international students are limited), **UWC South East Asia**, and other international schools (e.g. Dulwich, Tanglin, overseas-family schools).
- Feeds the **IB Diploma** application group at NUS/NTU. November-session IB results fit the normal cycle; May-session IB applicants at NUS get outcomes by the third week of July (official, NUS OAM).
- **Competitiveness (guidance):** competitive NUS/NTU programmes typically see admitted IB scores around 38–44/45; check IGP equivalents.

### 3e. Private schools / other routes
- Students in private education institutions often sit **Cambridge International A-Levels** or retake O-Levels privately; foreigners with such qualifications apply under **International Qualifications for Foreigners**, alongside Gaokao holders applying directly from China.
- **Guidance:** the international-qualifications pool is the most competitive per place for foreigners; outcomes are released latest (around July), and tuition-grant/fee implications differ.

## 4. Choosing a route (general guidance, not official)

| Student profile | Suggested route |
|---|---|
| Arrives by ~age 12–14, strong academics | AEIS → MOE secondary → O-Level → JC → A-Level |
| Arrives ~15–16, exam-oriented | Private O/A-Level prep or international school IB |
| Applied/technical learner | O-Level → Polytechnic → NUS/NTU (needs high GPA) |
| Exceptional in math/science, arrives early | NUS High School (very selective) |
| Family can afford international school | IB Diploma route |
| Stays in China through Gaokao | International Qualifications group (hardest pool) |
